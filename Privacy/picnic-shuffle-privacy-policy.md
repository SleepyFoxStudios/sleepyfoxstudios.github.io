## PRIVACY POLICY — Picnic Shuffle

**Effective date:** [EFFECTIVE DATE]

### 1. Who we are
Picnic Shuffle ("the app") is a puzzle game made by Sleepy Fox Studios [LEGAL ENTITY NAME] ("we", "us"). If you have questions about this policy, email us at [CONTACT EMAIL]. Postal address: [MAILING ADDRESS (optional)].

### 2. The short version
- **We don't collect your personal information.** The app has no accounts and no sign-in, and we don't run servers that receive your data.
- **The app shows ads from Google AdMob.** Google collects some data from your device to show ads, measure them, and prevent fraud. Section 4 explains what it collects.
- **You choose.** Apple's tracking prompt and Google's consent message let you decide how your data is used for ads. You can change those choices at any time.
- **Your game progress stays on your device.**

### 3. Information we collect
We, Sleepy Fox Studios, don't collect, receive, store, or sell any personal information from the app. Your progress, settings, and your "No Ads" purchase status are saved **only on your device** (see Section 7).

If you email us, we'll receive your email address and whatever you write. We use it only to reply to you, and we delete it once your question is resolved unless we need to keep it to meet a legal obligation.

### 4. Information collected by Google AdMob
The app uses Google AdMob, operated by Google LLC (or Google Ireland Ltd. for users in the EEA and UK), to show interstitial ads between levels and optional rewarded ads (for example, watching an ad to get a hint or +5 moves). When ads load, the Google Mobile Ads SDK may collect:
- **Device identifiers.** This includes the Apple advertising identifier (IDFA), but only if you allow tracking in Apple's prompt. It can also include app- or developer-scoped identifiers.
- **IP address.** It can be used to estimate your general (coarse) location, such as your country or city. The app doesn't use GPS or precise location.
- **Ad and usage data.** For example, which ads you saw or tapped, video views, and app launches.
- **Diagnostic data.** For example, crash logs and performance data such as launch time.

Google uses this data to show ads and measure them, for analytics, and to detect fraud and abuse. When you've given permission, it can also personalize ads. Google handles this data under its own policies. See "How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services": https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites and the Google Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy.

We require our advertising partner to protect your data at least as well as this policy describes, and we only work with partners that commit to doing so.

### 5. Your choices: tracking and ad consent
- **Apple's tracking prompt (App Tracking Transparency).** On iOS, the app asks whether it may track you across apps and websites owned by other companies. If you choose **Ask App Not to Track**, Google won't receive your IDFA and you'll still see ads, but they'll be less personalized. You can change this any time in **iPhone Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking**, or in **Settings > Picnic Shuffle**.
- **Google's consent message (EEA, UK, Switzerland).** In these regions, a Google consent message asks for your choices before personalized ads or certain data uses happen. You can accept, reject, or manage the options.
- **US state privacy choices.** In some US states, the same Google message lets you opt out of the "sale" or "sharing" of your personal information for targeted advertising.
- **Changing your mind.** Open **Settings > Privacy options** in the app. Where the law requires it, this button reopens Google's message so you can review or withdraw your choices.
- **Rewards never depend on these choices.** Rewarded ads, hints, and +5 moves work the same whether or not you allow tracking or personalized ads.

### 6. Purchases
The "No Ads" upgrade is sold through Apple's App Store. **Apple processes the payment**, and we never see or receive your card or payment details. The app gets only a confirmation from Apple that the purchase is valid, and it saves that on your device so ads stay off. To restore the purchase on a new device, use **Restore Purchases** in Settings. Apple's handling of your payment is covered by Apple's privacy policy: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/.

### 7. Game saves stored on your device
Your progress, stars, coins, settings, and No Ads status are stored locally on your device using the game engine's standard storage. They aren't sent to us. **Deleting the app deletes this data.** Because we don't have a copy, we can't restore it if it's lost.

### 8. Children
Picnic Shuffle is a general-audience game. It **isn't directed to children under 13** (or the equivalent minimum age where you live), and we don't knowingly collect personal information from children. For example, we can set ad requests to Google's child-directed treatment (non-personalized, family-safe ads).

### 9. Your rights
**EEA, UK, and Switzerland.** You have the right to access, correct, and delete your personal data, to restrict or object to its processing, to data portability, and to withdraw your consent at any time. Withdrawing consent doesn't affect processing that happened before. Personalized advertising and the storing or reading of information on your device rely on your **consent**, which you give or refuse in Google's consent message. Some ad measurement and fraud prevention may rely on **legitimate interests**, as described in Google's message. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

**US state residents** (for example, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia, and other states with privacy laws). Depending on where you live, you may have the right to know what personal information is collected, to delete or correct it, and to **opt out of the "sale" or "sharing" of personal information for targeted (cross-context behavioral) advertising**. We don't sell personal information for money and we don't collect it ourselves. Even so, allowing Google to use device identifiers for personalized ads may count as "sharing" or "selling" under some state laws. To opt out, choose **Ask App Not to Track**, use **Settings > Privacy options** in the app, or email us. We'll honor opt-out signals where the law requires it, and we won't treat you differently for using your rights.

Because we don't hold your data, most requests about ad data are best handled by Google: https://myadcenter.google.com and https://policies.google.com/privacy. We'll still help with any request sent to [CONTACT EMAIL].

We don't keep app data. Emails you send us are kept only as long as we need them to answer you. Google keeps the data it collects under its own retention policies: https://policies.google.com/technologies/retention.

### 11. Security
We don't store your data on our own systems, so there's nothing on our side for anyone to breach. Data sent to Google is transmitted using industry-standard encryption. No system is perfectly secure, so please keep your device's software up to date.

If we change this policy, we'll update the effective date at the top of this page. If the changes are significant, such as adding a new data partner, we'll also give notice in the app or the App Store release notes.

## Suggested App Store privacy-label answers (App Store Connect > App Privacy)

These answers are based on Google's AdMob iOS data disclosure, which lists IP address/coarse location, crash logs, performance data, device ID, advertising data, and product interactions. Apple counts an ad SDK as a "third-party partner," so its data has to be declared. Check this list against Google's current page and the SDK's privacy manifest (`PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`) and Xcode's privacy report for the exact SDK version you ship.

**Top-level answer: "Yes, we (or our third-party partners) collect data from this app."**

| Data type (Apple category) | Collected? | Linked to user? | Used for tracking? | Purposes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identifiers > **Device ID** (IDFA, IDFV/app-scoped IDs) | Yes | Yes | **Yes** (IDFA is the main tracking signal and is only sent when ATT is allowed) | Third-Party Advertising, Analytics |
| Location > **Coarse Location** (from IP) | Yes | Yes | Yes (conservative; see note) | Third-Party Advertising, Analytics |
| Usage Data > **Advertising Data** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Third-Party Advertising, Analytics |
| Usage Data > **Product Interaction** | Yes | Yes | Yes (conservative) | Third-Party Advertising, Analytics |
| Diagnostics > **Crash Data** | Yes | **No** (Google: "non-user related crash logs") | No | Analytics, App Functionality; Google also says diagnostics may be used for advertising, so consider adding Third-Party Advertising |
| Diagnostics > **Performance Data** | Yes | Yes (Google: "user-associated") | No / Yes (conservative: Yes, because Google says it may be shared with other entities that display ads) | Analytics, Third-Party Advertising |
| Diagnostics > Other Diagnostic Data | Only if the SDK manifest declares it | — | — | — |
| Purchases > **Purchase History** | **No** (confirm) | — | — | Apple processes the payment. The app stores only an on-device entitlement. Apple: data processed only on device isn't "collected," and "you are not responsible for disclosing data collected by Apple" |
| Financial Info > **Payment Info** | **No** | — | — | Apple: if payment is entered outside your app and you never have access to it, it "is not collected" |
| User Content > **Gameplay Content** | **No** | — | — | Saves stay in PlayerPrefs on the device and are never transmitted. Apple's on-device rule applies. Answer Yes if you ever add iCloud or cloud saves |
| Contact Info > Email | No | — | — | Support email only. This usually fits Apple's "optional disclosure" exception for customer-service requests, but confirm |
| All other categories | No | — | — | — |

**How the answers depend on ATT and settings**
- The label covers the **whole app**, not the choice an individual user makes. Because the app *asks* for ATT and sends the IDFA when a user allows it, answer **"Used for tracking: Yes"** for Device ID and the ad data linked to it. That's also why the app needs the ATT prompt.
- If you **never request ATT** and don't use personalized ads, set Google to non-personalized and limited ads. Most rows can then change to "Not used for tracking," and you can drop the ATT prompt. Confirm with Google's current guidance before you choose this.
- If you **turn on child-directed treatment (TFCD)** for everyone, Google doesn't send the IDFA. Device ID tracking would then be No, but the other collection (IP, diagnostics) still happens.
- The privacy policy URL is required. You can also add a "Privacy Choices" URL that points to Section 5.
- If **Unity Ads stays in the build**, add its data to the label as well, using Unity's own disclosure.

## Needs a real attorney

1. **Entity and jurisdiction.** Is Sleepy Fox Studios a registered LLC or company, or a trade name for Charlie personally? Which state or country is it in? That decides the legal name in the policy, personal liability, which laws apply, and whether any registrations are needed. (The store plan's example bundle ID uses `cleverfoxgaming`, which is a different name.)
2. **Kids audience and COPPA.** The FTC's "directed to children" test looks at visual content, animated characters, and child-oriented activities. Cute animals in a simple puzzle game could tip it, and COPPA treats a persistent identifier used for behavioral ads as personal information. A lawyer should decide whether the game is general audience, mixed audience (which needs a neutral age screen and TFCD/TFUA for under-13s), or child-directed (no behavioral ads, no IDFA, and Apple Kids Category limits). They should also confirm the Section 8 wording and the App Store age rating and marketing. Penalties run up to $53,088 per violation.
3. **CCPA/CPRA "sale/share" wording.** Do personalized AdMob ads count as "sharing" for cross-context behavioral advertising? If so, is a "Do Not Sell or Share" link or GPC handling needed on the website? Does a Google Restricted Data Processing/US-states message cover it? Note that the CCPA applies only above certain thresholds (more than $25M revenue, 100,000 or more consumers, or 50% of revenue from selling data), so it may not apply yet. Other state laws have their own thresholds.
4. **GDPR Article 27 EU/UK representative.** A non-EU controller targeting EU/UK users usually has to appoint a representative in writing unless the "occasional, low-risk" exemption applies. Get advice on whether showing AdMob ads to EU users qualifies, or whether to limit the EU/UK release. Also check whether we are a joint controller with Google for ad personalization.
5. **Terms of use / EULA.** Decide whether to rely on Apple's standard EULA or write custom terms with a governing-law clause, an arbitration clause and class-action waiver (enforceability varies, and consumer law limits these in the EU/UK), virtual-currency/coins wording, and refunds (Apple handles these).
6. **Legal bases.** Check the consent versus legitimate-interest split in Section 9 against the actual purposes configured in the AdMob GDPR message.
7. **Security and breach notice wording.** Some states require specific breach-notice language even when you hold little data.
